## The consultation

> **User:** My landlord in Pune is not returning my security deposit of 45000. I moved out on 15 March 2026 and he keeps saying next week. I have the rent agreement and the bank transfer receipt.

So your landlord is holding back a deposit of ₹45,000, and you moved out on 15 March 2026. Let me set out where that leaves you.

## Where you stand — ₹45,000

### ⏳ Your deadline

🟢 **You have about 860 days left.**

You have **3 years** from **15 March 2026** (the date you handed over the house, when the deposit became payable). That runs out on **15 March 2029**.

### 🏛️ Where to go

**Try these first — they are free and often enough:**

- **Written demand to the landlord** — Message or email the landlord with the amount, the date you vacated and a deadline. Get the refusal in writing if you can; it is the most useful document you will have.
  - Usually takes: 15 to 30 days
- **Legal notice** — Send by registered post with acknowledgement due, and keep the receipt and the tracking record.
  - Usually takes: 15 to 30 days to comply

**If that does not work:**

- **Rent Controller or Rent Tribunal under your state's rent law** — Varies by state; varies widely; you can do this yourself
  - Check whether your state's Rent Act gives a Rent Controller jurisdiction over deposit disputes. The District Legal Services Authority can tell you for free.
  - ⚠️ Whether this route exists at all depends entirely on your state.
- **Court of Small Causes** — Court fee on the amount claimed; lower than a regular suit; months; you can do this yourself
  - A summary money recovery suit, where your state's small causes ceiling covers the amount.
- **Civil suit for recovery of money** — Court fee on a percentage of the amount claimed, varying by state; a year or more; lawyer recommended
  - A recovery suit in the civil court with jurisdiction over the property or the landlord's residence.

### 📄 What to gather

**You already have:**

- ✅ the signed rent agreement
- ✅ proof that you paid (bank entry, receipt or bill)

Anything in writing where they admit the amount is your strongest card. Bank records beat memory. Save the screenshots now.

### ✅ What to do next

1. Put **15 March 2029** in your phone calendar now. That is your hard deadline.
2. Message or email the landlord with the amount, the date you vacated and a deadline. Get the refusal in writing if you can; it is the most useful document you will have.
3. Save your evidence somewhere it cannot be lost: screenshot the chats, download the bank statement, photograph the agreement.
4. If there is no reply in 15 days, send a written legal notice. This tool can draft one for you from the facts above.
5. If that fails: Rent Controller or Rent Tribunal under your state's rent law.

Sir / Madam,

1. The Sender was a tenant of the premises owned by the Addressee, and paid a refundable security deposit of ₹45,000. The Sender vacated the premises and handed over peaceful possession on 15 March 2026. Despite the tenancy having ended and repeated requests, the Addressee has failed and neglected to refund the said security deposit.

2. The Addressee's conduct is a breach of the obligations owed to the Sender. When a contract is broken, the party who suffers is entitled to receive compensation for any loss or damage caused which naturally arose in the usual course of things from the breach, or which the parties knew when they made the contract would be likely to result from it. Compensation is not given for remote or indirect loss. (Indian Contract Act 1872, s. 73)

3. The Sender hereby calls upon the Addressee to pay the sum of ₹45,000 within **15 days** of receipt of this notice, that is on or before **21 November 2026**.

4. Should the Addressee fail to comply within the said period, the Sender shall be constrained to initiate appropriate proceedings before the competent forum for recovery of the said amount, together with interest, compensation and costs, entirely at the risk and expense of the Addressee.

5. A copy of this notice is retained for record.

Yours faithfully,

  
[Your signature and name]

**How to send it**

- Print two copies. Sign both. Keep one.
- Send by **registered post with acknowledgement due** from any post office. Keep the receipt and the tracking slip — that receipt is your proof of service and matters as much as the notice itself.
- Email a scanned copy as well, if you have an address for them. Two routes, two proofs.
- Diarise **21 November 2026**. If nothing has arrived by then, move to the next step.

> ⏳ Sending a notice does **not** pause your legal deadline of **15 March 2029**. If they ignore you, do not keep waiting past that date.
